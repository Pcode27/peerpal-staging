Recommendation: Credits + freemium floor (what you already have) — but it needs structural tightening.

The credits model is correct for your market. M-Pesa micropayments are culturally normal in Kenya and credits lower the psychological barrier vs. "paying KSh 300 for therapy." However, right now credits and therapist session fees are entirely separate systems — you're implicitly running two models (credits + per-session marketplace) without clear logic connecting them. That needs to be articulated.

2. Tier Design Assessment

Current packages:

┌───────────────┬─────────┬─────────┬─────────────┐
│    Package    │  Price  │ Credits │ AI Sessions │
├───────────────┼─────────┼─────────┼─────────────┤
│ Just for now  │ KSh 150 │ 10      │ 4           │
├───────────────┼─────────┼─────────┼─────────────┤
│ I'm committed │ KSh 300 │ 25      │ 10          │
├───────────────┼─────────┼─────────┼─────────────┤
│ All of me     │ KSh 500 │ 50      │ 20          │
└───────────────┴─────────┴─────────┴─────────────┘

Issues:

- No upgrade trigger is designed in. A user on 10 credits has no in-product moment that nudges them toward 25. The packages are presented as equivalent options, not a ladder.
- The "I'm committed" package is the best value (10 credits for KSh 30/credit vs. 15 credits for KSh 20/credit) but nothing on the screen communicates that math. Users default to the cheapest.
- No free tier with a hard wall. The 1 free AI session/week + 2 welcome credits is a soft free tier, but it's invisible as positioning. Users don't feel like they're on "Free" — they just feel like they're out of credits.
- AI session allocation is not driving upgrades. 4 AI sessions per purchase is probably enough for most users in a month, removing the push to buy the larger bundle.
- Therapist booking is priced outside the credits system (KSh 1,500–4,000 direct). This is correct for high-ticket sessions but creates a two-track experience with no logical bridge between them.

Recommendation for tiers (not implementing — assessment only):

Rename and restructure around identity, not quantity:

- Free: 1 AI session/week, crisis tools, breathing exercises. Hard wall: no peer sessions.
- Starter — KSh 199: 8 peer credits, 3 AI sessions. Upgrade trigger: credits run out within 2–3 peer sessions.
- Regular — KSh 399: 20 peer credits, 8 AI sessions. Upgrade trigger: frequent users who want a therapist referral (costs 1 credit, but therapist session is separate).
- Committed — KSh 699: 40 peer credits, 15 AI sessions, 1 therapist referral credit included. Upgrade trigger: users already in therapy who want more peer support between sessions.

Price-per-credit stays roughly constant (KSh 17–19) but the Committed tier includes a therapist referral credit as a bonus — that's the decoy/anchor that makes it feel premium.

4. Unit Economics Check

Estimated cost per active member per month:

┌───────────────────────────┬─────────────────────────┬────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│         Cost item         │        Estimate         │                     Basis                      │
├───────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────┼────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ Supabase (DB + storage)   │ ~KSh 1.50/user          │ Pro plan ~$25/mo ÷ estimated 1,000 MAU         │
├───────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────┼────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ TURN server (video relay) │ ~KSh 8–15/voice session │ Metered; ~$0.05–0.10/min × 30 min              │
├───────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────┼────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ AI API (Claude/OpenAI)    │ ~KSh 5–12/conversation  │ ~$0.03–0.08 per session at current model costs │
├───────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────┼────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ Signaling server (VPS)    │ ~KSh 1/user             │ Fixed cost amortised                           │
├───────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────┼────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ Total per active user     │ ~KSh 15–30/mo           │ Estimate — TURN and AI costs unconfirmed       │
└───────────────────────────┴─────────────────────────┴────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

Gross margin check:

- A user buying KSh 150 (10 credits) who uses 3 peer text sessions + 2 AI sessions costs roughly KSh 15–30 to serve → margin ~80–90% (high because peer sessions have near-zero marginal cost; peers are users, not paid staff)
- Voice sessions cost more (TURN relay) — 2 credits = KSh 30 revenue at 15cr/KSh rate, but TURN cost may be KSh 8–15/session → margin compresses to 50–70% on voice
- Therapist sessions: PeerPal presumably takes a platform cut. If that cut is 10–15% of KSh 2,000–4,000, that's KSh 200–600 per booking with near-zero marginal cost → very high margin

Immediate concern: If TURN costs aren't capped per session, a long voice peer call eats the entire margin on that credit. Voice sessions at 2 credits need a hard 30-minute enforcement (which appears to be in place).

Break-even estimate: At KSh 300 average order value and ~85% gross margin, you need ~60 paying users/month to cover a KSh 15,000/mo infrastructure baseline. That's very achievable, but the number you need to watch is AI API cost as usage scales.

---
5. Pricing Psychology

Anchoring: The "I'm committed" package (KSh 300) should be the anchor — it's currently listed second but visually highlighted. That's correct. The name and the green highlight already do this.

Decoy: The "All of me" (KSh 500) exists but doesn't feel meaningfully different from "I'm committed" beyond raw volume. To work as a decoy that pushes people to the KSh 300 tier, "All of me" needs one exclusive feature (e.g., a free therapist referral credit, or priority matching) — otherwise it just reads as "more of the same for more money," and price-sensitive users skip it.

Annual framing: You don't currently offer annual plans. For a mental health platform this is worth considering — a KSh 2,500/year "Committed Annual" (vs. KSh 3,600 if paying monthly) creates commitment and improves cashflow. However it introduces complexity with M-Pesa (single large payment vs. recurring small ones). Probably not a priority until you have retention data.

---
6. Launch vs. Scale Pricing

Launch price assessment: Your current prices are already launch-level. KSh 150 for 10 credits (KSh 15/credit) is very cheap — a peer text session costs less than a chapati. This is intentional to drive adoption, but you should plan the scale price now so you don't get stuck.

Scale price target: KSh 25–30/credit (roughly 2× current). That means KSh 250/10cr, KSh 500/25cr, KSh 800/50cr. With Kenya's growing middle-class willingness to pay for mental health (Shamiri at KSh 2,500/mo found a market) this is reachable.

Grandfathering risk: You have no grandfathering policy. If you raise prices, anyone who bought credits under the old price keeps their existing credits (they've already paid), so there's no true grandfathering problem for credits — only for subscriptions. This is actually an advantage of the credits model.

Price increase triggers: Raise prices when:
1. Paid conversion rate exceeds 15% of registered users (demand proven)
2. TURN/AI costs are confirmed and margins are measured (not estimated)
3. You have a waitlist or full therapist availability — scarcity justifies price

The one structural risk: Peer earners receive credits (not cash). As the platform grows, high-volume peer supporters will accumulate large credit balances they can't redeem for money. This isn't a pricing problem now but becomes a liability once the platform has real scale. A credit-to-cash conversion path (even at a low rate) should be designed before you hit that wall.

---
Summary verdict: The bones are right. Credits model, M-Pesa, three tiers — all correct for the Kenyan market. The weakest points are (1) no designed upgrade trigger between tiers, (2) the "All of me" package doesn't earn its premium, and (3) no plan for the TURN cost cliff on voice sessions at scale. The therapist marketplace pricing (KSh 1,500–4,000) is well-positioned against the private market.
